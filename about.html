Our history

Two churches came together to form a third.

The present church came into being on 11 February 1959 as an amalgamation of Queen Street Baptist and Cape Road Baptist churches. Cape Road Baptist had been established in the 1930s and held services in the older building which is now the church hall.

The current larger building was constructed with the merging of the two churches in mind. Its pitched roof was designed to mimic two praying hands. The name Trinity reflects the thought that two churches were able to merge in order to form a third church.

In 2019, Trinity celebrated its 60th year with 150 members.

  1. 11 MAR 1958The idea of amalgamation between Queen Street Baptist and Cape Road Baptist is first raised by Rev. L. J. Gardner.
  2. 17–28 MAR 1958The two diaconates meet and both churches decide to amalgamate.
  3. 17 SEP 1958Rev. S. H. Reed accepts the call; he is inducted on 18 January 1959.
  4. 11 FEB 1959The amalgamation becomes an accomplished fact.
  5. 18 OCT 1959The first united service is conducted by the pastor in the hall.
  6. 30 NOV 1960A decision to build is made after a generous gift from Mr S. R. Siberry.
  7. 18 NOV 1961Mr S. R. Siberry opens the new church building.
The Tweed

A story carried in the church’s timber.

Trinity has a connection with a paddle steam ship, the Tweed, launched in Bombay in 1854. After damage in Algoa Bay, it was towed into Port Elizabeth Harbour in 1888 and its usable parts were auctioned off.

It is believed that beams from the ship were used to build the roof of a Port Elizabeth church, possibly Trinity. What is confirmed is that a Trinity communion table and chairs were made from its wood.
